Small cakes, big joy. Whether you love them minimal or maximal, one flavour or the whole spectrum—this is a love letter to diversity in a paper liner.

See the spectrum

Greatness looks different on everyone

Same idea—cake in a cup—endless personalities. Here are four moods; the real menu is infinite.

  • Plain & proud

    Golden crumb, honest butter and sugar, nothing to hide. The cupcake as comfort object—warm, familiar, quietly perfect.

  • Swirls of frosting

    Vanilla bean, chocolate ganache, cream cheese peaks. Texture meets colour; the first bite is architecture and the second is nostalgia.

  • Sprinkle party

    Confetti crunch on top of soft crumb—playful, loud, unapologetically fun. Proof that dessert can wear its personality on the outside.

  • All the colours

    Layered batters, ombré buttercream, fruit and florals—cupcakes as tiny canvases. Every hue has a place at the table.

A short history of the cupcake

From measure-by-cup to global icon—small cakes with a long story.

  1. 18th–19th c.

    Measured in cups

    The name likely comes from recipes measured by volume—cup cake—before standardized scales were common. Home bakers could repeat success without fancy equipment.

  2. Early 1900s

    Enter the liner

    Mass-produced paper cups and hostess-style cakes helped cupcakes travel: portable, portioned, and giftable. The “personal cake” took shape in lunchboxes and bake sales.

  3. 2000s

    Boutique boom

    Specialty shops turned the cupcake into a design object—tall swirls, novel flavours, and queues around the block. Suddenly “just a cupcake” was a statement.

  4. Today

    Everyone’s medium

    From minimalist naturals to maximalist rainbows, cupcakes carry culture, identity, and joy. The format stayed small; the imagination didn’t.

The future is still sweet

Where small-batch baking and big ideas might go next—speculative, hopeful, and a little hungry.

Kind to the planet

Compostable liners, local grain, less waste in decoration—future cupcakes might wear their ethics as proudly as their sprinkles.

Flavours without borders

Global pantries—spices, teas, tropical fruits—keep remixing the base recipe. The “classic” cupcake keeps gaining new classics.

Inclusion by design

Allergen-aware kitchens and clear labelling mean more people get to say yes. Diversity isn’t only visual—it’s who gets invited to the party.

Still handmade

Even with new tools, the magic stays human: a steady hand on the piping bag, a friend’s favourite flavour remembered. Some futures don’t need reinventing.
